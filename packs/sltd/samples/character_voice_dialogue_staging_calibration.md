# SLTD Character Voice / Dialogue / Staging Calibration

These samples calibrate voice distinction, dialogue weight, relationship memory, multi-character presence, location staging, and tone integrity.

They are not canon, not current manuscript, and not prose to imitate.

## 1. Dialogue should not be chapter function in costume

```text
BAD PATTERN:
Every character says the exact information the scene needs.

WHY BAD:
- same voice
- static talk
- dialogue carries outline labels instead of life

PREFERRED CALIBRATION:
Thúy Hoa speaks through care, work, and fear.
Đại Sơn speaks shorter when calculating survival.
Lão Hoắc withholds words he knows too well.
Bà Tư may cut through softness with price and habit.
```

## 2. Silence must be active

```text
BAD PATTERN:
A character present in the room disappears because the scene focuses on two speakers.

WHY BAD:
- multi-character staging breaks
- silence has no pressure

PREFERRED CALIBRATION:
WHO STAYS SILENT: Bà Tư
WHY SILENT: she is counting who heard the line, or deciding whether to spend authority
PRESENCE TRACE: cup placed harder, eyes moved to the door, hand stopped over medicine
```

## 3. Relationship memory is shown by address and handling

```text
BAD PATTERN:
The narration says two characters care about each other.

WHY BAD:
- relationship is announced, not earned

PREFERRED CALIBRATION:
A character changes how they call the other, leaves water closer, does not ask the question everyone else asks, or hides a cost without naming care.
```

## 4. Location is a stage, not backdrop

```text
BAD PATTERN:
The scene happens by the well, but the well does not affect speaking, standing, waiting, or witnessing.

WHY BAD:
- place is decorative
- social pressure loses body

PREFERRED CALIBRATION:
LOCATION LAYOUT: well mouth, rope, wet stone, queue, fence, dog, lane bend
USABLE OBJECTS: bucket, rope, water mark, mud print
ENVIRONMENT PRESSURE: everyone hears who is called and who is skipped
```

## 5. Tone can hold humor without breaking stakes

```text
BAD PATTERN:
A joke appears only to lighten the mood.

WHY BAD:
- tone breaks
- character voice becomes external

PREFERRED CALIBRATION:
Humor comes from the speaker being exactly themselves: a child bargaining badly, an old woman snapping at waste, a tired hunter answering too dryly. The joke must still belong to the danger, debt, or relation in the scene.
```

## 6. Sound should fit SLTD prose

```text
BAD PATTERN:
Bold-caps comic sound effects are inserted into SLTD action.

WHY BAD:
- style mismatch
- folk-dread tone breaks

PREFERRED CALIBRATION:
Use natural Vietnamese sound and material response: rắc, phịch, cạch, soạt, bùn ục dưới chân, dây gai rít qua tay. Let sound sit inside the sentence.
```
