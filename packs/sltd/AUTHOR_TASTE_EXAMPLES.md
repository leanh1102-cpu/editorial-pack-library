# SLTD Author Taste Examples

This file gives concrete taste examples for SLTD editing.

It guides editing choices. It does not override user instruction, Notion live source, canon, scene function, evidence discipline, or current task scope.

## Hard dislikes

- Symbol-first prose: a scene is forced into meaning before bodies, objects, and actions make it real.
- Same-voice dialogue: characters speak with the same rhythm, same clean logic, same level of explanation.
- Lore-frontloading: terms, systems, titles, and history arrive before the scene needs them.
- Outline labels showing on the page: a scene reads like it is following a card instead of happening.
- Safe but thin scene: canon is correct, but no one pays a cost.
- Clean transition habit: every paragraph opens and closes too neatly.
- Emotion naming without pressure: the prose says hurt, fear, grief, or loneliness before the page makes the reader feel it.
- Object as label: a prop is used to signal meaning, not to change what a character can do.

## Preferred prose behavior

- Ground emotion through hands, breath, body, object, sound, and silence.
- Let daily life cut across lore: medicine, sewing, food, tools, animal reaction, debt, illness, weather, hiding place.
- Let characters hide part of what they know.
- Let one scene carry one main pressure.
- Make a choice cost something visible.
- Keep mystery behind concrete pressure until the scene earns a reveal.
- Use short sentences when a character is cut off, cornered, or unable to say more.
- Use longer sentences only when the action has a real physical chain.

## Dialogue taste

Good dialogue should show difference in age, power, fear, work, and habit.

Avoid:

```text
All characters explaining the same idea with the same clean sentence shape.
```

Prefer:

```text
One character evades.
One character snaps.
One character asks the wrong question.
One character hears only half a sentence.
One character changes the subject because the room has become unsafe.
```

## Scene pressure taste

A good SLTD scene should answer:

```text
What changes in the room?
Who notices first?
Who cannot say the full truth?
What object becomes harder to ignore?
What must be done before the scene ends?
What cost follows the choice?
```

## Before / after pattern

### Too AI

```text
The cold stone seemed to carry an ancient secret, quietly connecting the family to a fate they could not escape.
```

Why it fails:

- names meaning before the scene earns it;
- uses abstract pressure;
- turns the object into a label;
- no body, no room, no choice.

### Closer to SLTD taste

```text
The stone sat under the stove leg. Thuy Hoa pushed the medicine bowl away, then pulled it back with two fingers. The yellow dog stopped scratching at the door.
```

Why it works:

- the object changes the room;
- the body reacts before anyone explains;
- daily life stays on the page;
- the dog gives pressure without lore.

## Repair moves

When a passage feels correct but thin, try one of these moves:

- add a physical task that becomes harder;
- make a character hear something wrong;
- make a prop force a choice;
- cut one explanation and replace it with action;
- let a character refuse to finish a sentence;
- bring back a small debt, illness, injury, tool, or animal reaction;
- make the scene end with a practical problem, not a slogan.

## C030 taste anchor

For C030-style material, keep lore behind:

```text
kitchen
medicine bowl
sewing needle
patched shirt
yellow dog
old paper
ash
breath
hiding choice
```

The stone should not arrive as a grand symbol. It should first make the house behave wrong.

## Editing rule

Author taste is a guide, not a license to invent.

If taste conflicts with current Notion source, canon, scene function, or user instruction, stop and ask for direction or return a narrow patch candidate.
