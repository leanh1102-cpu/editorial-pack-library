# KX9 — Modern Vietnamese Register

**Status:** `CANDIDATE-STABLE / VALIDATED — NARROW SOURCE SCOPE`

## CONCEPT
Modern Vietnamese register is the patterned adjustment of language to relation, role, setting, purpose, mode, formality, region, idiolect, and immediate speech condition. Literary prose may preserve, contrast, or manipulate these resources without treating one prestige variety as the universal literary norm.

Core route:

`SPEAKER/NARRATOR → ADDRESSEE/READER → RELATION/ROLE → SETTING/PURPOSE → MODE/FORMALITY → REGION/IDIOLECT → LOCAL FUNCTION → PRESERVE OR REPAIR`

## DEFINITION
- **Register:** a context-sensitive configuration of linguistic choices associated with communicative situation and function.
- **Mode:** the communicative channel/orientation, including written, speech-like, documentary, epistolary, conversational, or other textually represented modes.
- **Tenor/relation:** the social relation among participants, including status, intimacy, distance, obligation, institutional role, and audience.
- **Formality:** relative ceremonial, institutional, professional, neutral, colloquial, intimate, or rough positioning; not a simple quality ranking.
- **Regional variation:** forms associated with geographical/social communities whose function depends on character, narrator, setting, and audience.
- **Idiolect:** recurring individual speech tendencies of a particular character or narrator.
- **Speech condition:** temporary condition such as rehearsal, ceremony, shock, fatigue, threat, privacy, or intoxication that can shift surface register.
- **Prestige normalization:** replacing contextually functional language with a socially prestigious/standard variety merely because it appears more correct or literary.

## SCOPE
Use this module when reviewing or crafting modern Vietnamese prose/dialogue whose language varies by social relation, institutional role, formality, region, speaker identity, narrator stance, or situation.

Do not use it to:
- declare one prestige/standard variety the universal literary target;
- standardize stable dialect or idiolect without a demonstrated task-specific problem;
- treat colloquial language as inherently less literary;
- infer education, morality, intelligence, class, or region from one isolated lexical marker;
- certify historical authenticity or period-correct register;
- replace character-bound speech with narrator-neutral Vietnamese merely for smoothness.

## MECHANISM
### 1. Establish the communicative frame
Identify who speaks/narrates, to whom, under what relation, role, setting, and purpose.

### 2. Identify mode and formality
Ask whether the text is represented as conversation, formal statement, private speech, narration, document, ceremonial utterance, message, or another mode.

### 3. Track regional and idiolectal patterning
A regional form or habitual phrasing has stronger diagnostic value when it recurs consistently and remains tied to a character/narrator rather than appearing as random decoration.

### 4. Track stateful shifts
Prepared, ceremonial, threatened, intimate, exhausted, or shocked speech may diverge from baseline voice. Consistency is behavioral/contextual, not identical surface syntax in every scene.

### 5. Judge intelligibility and function
Variation may be preserved when it is sufficiently recoverable for the intended audience and carries character, relation, pressure, setting, or narrative function.

### 6. Standardize only for a demonstrated reason
Possible task-specific reasons include accidental inconsistency, unintended speaker confusion, inaccessible wording that blocks required understanding, or an explicit house/style request that does not destroy required voice.

## TEXTUAL SIGNALS
- honorific and address choices;
- complete/formal versus clipped/colloquial syntax;
- regional items such as `bển`, `má`, `hổng` when character-bound;
- stable idiom mistakes or low-education forms;
- institutional/ceremonial diction;
- speech-like particles and repairs;
- temporary register elevation in rehearsed speech;
- register collapse under shock or pressure;
- narrator/dialogue contrast;
- sudden unexplained shifts in diction unrelated to relation, role, state, or purpose.

## EFFECTS
Register can signal or shape:
- hierarchy and distance;
- intimacy and solidarity;
- institutional role;
- regional/social belonging;
- character differentiation;
- performance, ceremony, or self-presentation;
- pressure-driven state change;
- narrator stance and distance;
- irony or mismatch between social role and actual action.

No single marker establishes a complete social identity or literary value.

## CONDITIONS
Preserve register variation when:
- it is stable enough to be recognizable;
- it matches relation, role, purpose, region, idiolect, or speech condition;
- readers can recover the required meaning;
- normalization would erase character/social information;
- the task does not explicitly require a different house standard with higher priority.

Repair when:
- variation is random rather than patterned;
- speaker identity becomes materially unclear;
- a supposed dialect marker is inconsistent or decorative without contextual support;
- formality shifts break established behavioral logic without a plausible condition;
- wording blocks required comprehension and a smaller clarification can preserve voice.

## COUNTEREXAMPLES
- A district clerk using full honorific sentences and a dock worker using clipped colloquial replies need not be made consistent with each other.
- Stable `bển`, `má`, `hổng` in one southern character's dialogue are not errors merely because narration uses a different variety.
- A rough speaker may deliver a smoother rehearsed memorial oath and then return to baseline speech.
- A character's recurring malformed idiom can remain when it is stable, intelligible, and character-bound.
- A formal narrator can quote colloquial dialogue without the novel having an inconsistent register.

## COMMON ERRORS
- prestige normalization;
- same-voice smoothing;
- equating colloquiality with poor writing;
- treating regional words as noise to delete;
- stereotyping a character from one marker;
- forcing narrator and dialogue into the same register;
- treating prepared speech as a continuity error;
- adding dialect spellings randomly to simulate authenticity;
- assuming standardization always improves readability.

## HISTORICAL VARIATION
`SOURCE-GATED`.

This module concerns modern Vietnamese register only. Historical honorifics, lexical values, official titles, orthography, and period-specific social language require dated textual/source evidence and belong to historical/register modules. If the task asks whether a form is period-correct, route to KX10 or HOLD when evidence is missing.

## FORM VARIATION
Different modern forms—dialogue-heavy fiction, documentary fiction, letters/messages, oral-style narration, memoir, formal documents, lyric/prose hybrids—may distribute register differently.

Form-specific literary conventions remain source-gated where the package lacks authoritative coverage. Do not turn observations from one form into universal prose law.

## CLOSE READING METHOD
Map:

`WHO → TO WHOM → RELATION/ROLE → SETTING/PURPOSE → MODE → FORMALITY → REGION/IDIOLECT → SPEECH CONDITION → EFFECT → MATERIAL PROBLEM?`

Then ask whether the proposed edit preserves the communicative/social information carried by the register.

## CRAFT TRANSFER
Transfer the contextual mechanism rather than lexical tokens.

Examples:
- recreate status contrast using different roles and different linguistic markers;
- recreate prepared/baseline contrast with a different ceremony and character;
- recreate regional/idiolect differentiation using a different feature set instead of copying `bển/má/hổng`.

## DRILLS
1. Map one dialogue exchange by relation, role, purpose, formality, region, and speech condition.
2. Identify which of five register differences are functional versus accidental drift.
3. Preserve a regional voice while repairing one material ambiguity.
4. Write the same request under intimate, institutional, and adversarial relations without treating one as inherently better.
5. Diagnose a prepared-speech shift and its recovery to baseline.

## AI OPERATIONAL RULES
- No prestige variety is a universal literary norm.
- Standardization requires a demonstrated task-specific need.
- Preserve stable, intelligible dialect/idiolect when it carries character, relation, region, or social function.
- Do not enforce same-voice consistency across different speakers or different speech conditions.
- Treat register as context-sensitive: relation, role, setting, purpose, mode, formality, region, idiolect, and pressure matter.
- Do not infer social identity or psychological truth from one lexical marker.
- Modern-register competence does not grant historical authenticity judgments.
- If historical correctness is materially at issue, source-gate the verdict.

## SELF-CHECK
- Whose language is this: narrator, character, document, quoted speech?
- Who is the addressee/reader inside the scene or form?
- What relation, role, setting, and purpose apply?
- Is the variation stable or random?
- Am I treating prestige usage as quality by default?
- Would standardization erase useful voice/social information?
- Is a shift explained by preparation, ceremony, pressure, intimacy, or recovery?
- Am I making a historical claim beyond this module's scope?
