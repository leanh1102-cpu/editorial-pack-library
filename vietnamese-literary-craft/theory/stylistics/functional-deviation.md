# KX3 — Functional Deviation and Foregrounding

**Status:** `CANDIDATE-STABLE / VALIDATED`

## CONCEPT
Literary style often creates effect by departing from an expected local pattern or by intensifying a pattern through parallelism/repetition. A marked feature must therefore be judged relative to context and function, not by automatic normalization.

Core distinction:

`DEVIATION / PARALLELISM → ATTENTION → LOCAL FUNCTION? → PRESERVE OR REPAIR`

## DEFINITION
- **Foregrounding:** increased textual prominence produced by deviation from expectation, patterning/parallelism, or other marked organization.
- **External deviation:** departure from a broader linguistic or genre expectation.
- **Internal deviation:** departure from a norm established by the text itself.
- **Markedness:** relative salience against a relevant background expectation.
- **Functional deviation:** a marked departure whose local effect is supported by textual evidence.
- **Accidental failure:** irregularity that produces unintended confusion, contradiction, loss of reference, or another demonstrated defect without compensating function.

Deviation is neither automatically good nor automatically bad.

## SCOPE
Use this module to assess unusual syntax, fragments, ellipsis, repetition, asymmetry, metaphorical strain, register shifts, sentence-length contrast, or other stylistic irregularity.

Do not use it to:
- excuse every error as artistic choice;
- treat every unusual feature as deep or symbolic;
- normalize every departure from standard grammar;
- infer historical authenticity from archaic-looking surface features;
- derive literary quality from frequency counts alone.

## MECHANISM
### 1. Establish the relevant norm
Ask what expectation the text itself or the relevant speech/register context establishes.

Examples:
- controlled complete syntax surrounding three shock fragments;
- long periodic sentences interrupted by one short refusal;
- a rough speaker shifting into rehearsed ceremonial diction;
- repeated lexical/syntactic pattern whose final instance breaks.

### 2. Identify the marked feature
Name the actual deviation or parallelism rather than calling prose merely `awkward`, `poetic`, or `strong`.

### 3. Test recoverability
Can a reader still recover reference, event sequence, communicative action, or intended semantic relation at the level the scene requires?

### 4. Test local function
Does the feature alter attention, rhythm, pressure, perception, social positioning, cognitive access, or another demonstrated effect?

### 5. Choose the smallest defensible action
- function supported and no material defect → `PRESERVE`;
- local accidental defect inside an otherwise functional structure → `MIN_EDIT`;
- larger mechanism failure → `REVISE`;
- historical/form/register judgment needs unavailable evidence → `HOLD-INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`.

## TEXTUAL SIGNALS
Potential foregrounding/deviation signals:
- fragments inside otherwise complete syntax;
- ellipsis or missing expected material;
- inversion/marked order;
- abrupt sentence-length change;
- syntactic parallelism or broken parallelism;
- lexical repetition with changing context;
- unusual metaphor or collocation;
- punctuation fracture;
- abrupt register shift;
- asymmetry in paragraph structure;
- a feature that violates an internal pattern established earlier in the text.

Signal detection is diagnostic only; it does not prove successful style.

## EFFECTS
Possible effects include:
- intensified attention;
- slowed or accelerated reading;
- perceptual fracture;
- pressure or hesitation;
- ceremonial or socially marked speech;
- defamiliarization;
- emphasis through pattern/repetition;
- expectation and surprise;
- unstable or layered interpretation.

Effect claims must be grounded in the local textual relation, not in the mere presence of unusual form.

## CONDITIONS
Preserve deviation when most of the following are true:
- the surrounding norm is identifiable;
- the departure is bounded or patterned;
- reference/meaning is sufficiently recoverable for scene function;
- placement corresponds to pressure, perception, voice, or another demonstrated change;
- normalization would remove a useful distinction or effect.

Repair deviation when:
- ambiguity is unintended and materially consequential;
- syntax obscures the intended relation without compensating function;
- repetition adds no new pressure, relation, rhythm, or information;
- register shift lacks a plausible condition and reads as accidental drift;
- metaphor/marked structure blocks rather than productively complicates the intended reading.

## COUNTEREXAMPLES
- `Khói. Tai ù. Cửa ở đâu?` after an explosion can be functional fracture against controlled surrounding syntax.
- Three repetitions of `Đừng` can be functional when each occurs at a changed action threshold.
- A 49-word sentence is not defective merely because it is long if every clause changes the active decision.
- A rough speaker's rehearsed memorial oath can legitimately differ from baseline speech.
- A pronoun with two plausible referents in a legal-action sentence is not protected as artistic ambiguity when the later plot requires a specific signer and the ambiguity is unintended.
- Random grammatical damage gains no artistic protection merely by being unusual.

## COMMON ERRORS
- `fragments are errors`;
- `repetition is weak writing`;
- `long sentences should be shortened`;
- `unusual syntax should be normalized`;
- `dialect should be standardized for literary quality`;
- `every roughness is intentional`;
- `every unusual feature is foregrounding`;
- counting recurrence as proof of symbolic importance;
- using `show, don't tell` as a universal correction rule.

## HISTORICAL VARIATION
`SOURCE-GATED`.

A form that appears deviant to a contemporary reader may be ordinary under another historical norm. Historical lexical, syntactic, rhetorical, or orthographic status requires relevant sources before declaring deviation/error.

## FORM VARIATION
`SOURCE-GATED BY FORM`.

Meter, parallelism, regulated syntax, rhyme, lineation, biền văn structure, or other form-specific expectations can establish norms against which deviation matters. Until the relevant form module/source is available, do not impose a guessed norm.

## CLOSE READING METHOD
Use the diagnostic sequence:

`LOCAL NORM → MARKED FEATURE → RECOVERABILITY → FUNCTION → COST OF NORMALIZATION → ACTION`

Questions:
1. What pattern or expectation exists immediately around the feature?
2. How exactly does the feature depart from or intensify it?
3. What happens to reading rhythm, attention, access, or social meaning?
4. Is the effect textually supported or only asserted?
5. Would normalization erase a useful contrast?
6. Is there a smaller repair that preserves the mechanism?

## CRAFT TRANSFER
Transfer the relation between norm and deviation, not the original surface feature.

Examples:
- If a source uses fragments after impact, a transfer can instead establish flowing syntax and break it at a moment of interrupted breath.
- If a source uses lexical repetition to escalate warning, a transfer can use syntactic parallelism or repeated gesture under changing stakes.

Do not copy the same metaphor, object, sentence pattern, or scene setup merely because it produced foregrounding in the source.

## DRILLS
1. **Norm detection:** identify the local norm before naming any deviation.
2. **Leave-alone test:** classify five marked features as `PRESERVE / MIN_EDIT / REVISE` with one-sentence functional evidence.
3. **False-positive test:** find an irregularity that is merely confusing rather than productive.
4. **Parallelism test:** distinguish repetition with escalation from duplication without changed function.
5. **Transfer test:** recreate foregrounding using a different stylistic device from the model.

## AI OPERATIONAL RULES
- Function before correction.
- Identify the relevant local/internal norm before judging deviation.
- Do not use grammar regularity, sentence length, repetition count, or lexical rarity as standalone quality tests.
- Preserve bounded fragments, ellipsis, roughness, repetition, or register shifts when their function is demonstrated and meaning remains sufficiently recoverable.
- Do not protect a material reference/access/continuity failure by labeling it poetic or ambiguous.
- When a local defect exists inside a functional marked structure, repair the defect without flattening the structure.
- Historical/form-specific deviation judgments remain source-gated.
- Frequency is evidence of recurrence, not proof of quality, intention, symbolism, or rule.

## SELF-CHECK
- What norm am I comparing this feature against?
- Is that norm actually established by text/context or assumed by me?
- What is marked here: deviation, parallelism, or both?
- What specific effect is supported?
- Is meaning/reference sufficiently recoverable?
- Would my edit erase pressure, voice, rhythm, or interpretive work?
- Am I excusing a genuine failure merely because the prose is unusual?
- Did I convert frequency into a literary law?
