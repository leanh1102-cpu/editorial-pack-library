# KX6 — Dialogue Craft

**Status:** `CANDIDATE-STABLE / VALIDATED`

## CONCEPT
Dialogue is social action performed through language under specific relations, goals, constraints, and pressure. Literary dialogue should therefore be judged by what speakers are trying to do to or with one another, not by whether their sentences share one polished register.

Core route:

`SPEAKER GOAL → ADDRESSEE → RELATION/STATUS → PRESSURE → UTTERANCE → IMPLICATION/WITHHOLDING → RESPONSE → RECOVERY`

## DEFINITION
- **Speaker goal:** the practical, relational, defensive, persuasive, evasive, ceremonial, or informational purpose motivating a turn.
- **Addressee model:** what the speaker assumes, fears, wants, or expects from the listener.
- **Social action:** what an utterance does in interaction beyond its literal proposition: requesting, refusing, warning, accusing, placating, testing, stalling, aligning, etc.
- **Speech condition:** situational factors such as rehearsal, ceremony, intoxication, shock, hierarchy, privacy, fatigue, or threat that can alter surface voice.
- **Withholding:** information or response deliberately not supplied despite communicative relevance.
- **Recovery:** return toward baseline speech after a temporary pressure- or situation-driven register shift.

## SCOPE
Use this module to diagnose or craft dialogue involving indirect action, hierarchy, conflict, persuasion, evasion, differentiated voice, interruption, silence, prepared speech, or state-dependent register change.

Do not use it to:
- normalize all characters toward one literary standard;
- infer hidden intention as fact when the scene only supports a plausible reading;
- convert direct thought into spoken dialogue merely for liveliness;
- force every character to display a fixed surface voice in every situation;
- correct stable dialect/idiolect solely because it departs from prestige usage.

## MECHANISM
### 1. Identify the social task of the turn
Ask what the speaker is attempting: obtain action, protect face, threaten, conceal, recruit, deflect, confess, delay, or maintain ritual/social order.

### 2. Map speaker–addressee relation
Status, intimacy, obligation, conflict history, role, and audience affect what can be said directly and what is safer to imply.

### 3. Separate literal content from plausible force
A declarative can plausibly function as a request or warning. Preserve the distinction between what was explicitly said and what the listener/reader may infer.

### 4. Track pressure and speech condition
Voice is stateful. A rough speaker may deliver rehearsed ceremonial language; a formal speaker may briefly drop register under surprise and recover later.

### 5. Read silence, interruption, and withholding as actions only when context supports them
Silence may refuse, conceal, concede, punish, process, or simply mark uncertainty. Do not assign one meaning automatically.

### 6. Preserve differentiated voices
Variation can follow region, education, role, relation, preparation, immediate state, and social purpose. Consistency means behavioral logic, not identical syntax across all contexts.

## TEXTUAL SIGNALS
- indirect declaratives near actionable situations;
- abrupt refusals or repeated warnings;
- honorific differences;
- clipped versus elaborated turns;
- interruption, overlap, self-repair, hesitation;
- stable regional or idiolectal forms;
- temporary ceremonial/formal speech;
- register collapse under surprise or threat followed by recovery;
- topic shifts used to evade or control;
- unreturned questions or strategic silence.

## EFFECTS
Dialogue choices can create:
- hierarchy and resistance;
- intimacy or distance;
- conflict and negotiation;
- indirect pressure;
- character differentiation;
- concealment and reader inference;
- comic or tragic mismatch between literal and social meaning;
- state change visible through register shift.

Effects require interactional evidence; one lexical marker alone does not prove a complete social role or psychology.

## CONDITIONS
Preserve dialogue variation when:
- the speaker/addressee relation supports it;
- regional/idiolectal features are stable and intelligible enough for the task;
- register shift matches rehearsal, ceremony, shock, threat, or another established condition;
- repetition/interruption changes pressure or interactional position;
- indirectness remains inferable without redundant explanation.

Repair when:
- the turn cannot plausibly serve the intended social action;
- voice changes without any established condition and breaks behavioral logic;
- attribution/reference makes speaker identity unclear materially;
- exposition is placed in dialogue without a plausible addressee reason;
- dialect markers become random decoration rather than stable voice behavior.

## COUNTEREXAMPLES
- `Ở đây gió lùa quá.` may function as an indirect request, but the request is not explicit fact.
- A district clerk and dock worker can legitimately use different syntax/register in the same exchange.
- A rough speaker can produce smoother language in a rehearsed memorial oath and then return to baseline.
- `Đừng. Đừng mở. Đừng mở cái cửa đó.` can be functional escalation rather than redundant repetition.
- Stable `bển`, `má`, `hổng` in one character's dialogue need not be standardized when intelligible and contextually grounded.
- Direct interior thought remains thought unless the scene establishes actual utterance.

## COMMON ERRORS
- same-voice smoothing;
- prestige normalization of dialect/idiolect;
- treating prepared speech as voice inconsistency;
- making every indirect intention explicit;
- explaining silence inside narration before the scene supplies evidence;
- using one catchphrase as the entire character voice;
- adding filler merely to make dialogue sound natural;
- turning thought into speech for pace;
- treating visible emotion as identical to inner emotion.

## HISTORICAL VARIATION

Honorific systems, forms of address, speech conventions, and lexical/register values vary historically. Do not certify period-appropriate dialogue or titles without relevant period/institution/source evidence.

## FORM VARIATION
Dialogue in stage drama, oral-style narrative, epistolary form, classical prose, documentary fiction, and lyric forms can follow different conventions. Form-specific rules remain source-gated until the relevant module exists.

## CLOSE READING METHOD
For each important turn, map:

`WHO SPEAKS → TO WHOM → WHAT IS SAID → WHAT ACTION IS PLAUSIBLE → STATUS/RELATION/PRESSURE → WHAT IS WITHHELD → RESPONSE → CHANGE IN POSITION`

Keep explicit wording separate from inferred social action.

## CRAFT TRANSFER
Transfer interactional mechanics rather than phrases.

Examples:
- recreate an indirect request using a different setting, relation, and literal statement;
- recreate escalating refusal with different wording and action thresholds;
- recreate prepared/baseline contrast using a different ceremony and character profile.

## DRILLS
1. Label ten dialogue turns by literal content and plausible social action separately.
2. Rewrite one exchange to preserve status difference without caricature.
3. Build a three-turn escalation where repetition changes pressure each time.
4. Write a prepared speech that differs from baseline voice but remains behaviorally plausible.
5. Diagnose three silences without assigning intention unless the surrounding evidence supports it.

## AI OPERATIONAL RULES
- Dialogue is judged through speaker goal, addressee, relation, status, pressure, and social purpose.
- Explicit content and implicature remain distinct.
- Preserve dialect/idiolect when stable, intelligible, and character-bound unless a task-specific readability problem is demonstrated.
- Do not enforce one surface voice across preparation, shock, ceremony, fatigue, intimacy, or recovery states.
- Preserve functional interruption, roughness, repetition, and silence when their interactional work is demonstrated.
- Do not infer deception, motive, or inner emotion from one turn alone.
- Do not convert direct thought into spoken dialogue without evidence that it is uttered.

## SELF-CHECK
- What does the speaker want from this addressee now?
- What is explicit, and what am I inferring?
- How do relation/status/pressure shape the turn?
- Is the voice difference stable or condition-driven?
- Did I normalize dialect or roughness merely for polish?
- Does repetition/interruption change the interaction?
- Did I assign a meaning to silence without evidence?
- Did I preserve thought/speech boundaries?
