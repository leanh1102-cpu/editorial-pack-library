# KX4 — Sentence Craft

**Status:** `CANDIDATE-STABLE / VALIDATED`

## CONCEPT
Sentence craft is the control of information, relation, rhythm, reference, compression, and emphasis at sentence scale. A sentence is judged by what its structure does in context, not by a preferred length or a generic smoothness target.

Core route:

`INFORMATION → CLAUSE RELATION → REFERENCE → RHYTHM → MEDIATION → FUNCTION → SMALLEST REPAIR`

## DEFINITION
- **Information order:** the sequence in which given, new, contrastive, causal, or consequential material is presented.
- **Clause relation:** how clauses or phrases relate temporally, causally, conditionally, contrastively, additively, or by elaboration.
- **Reference:** how names, pronouns, noun phrases, and ellipsis identify entities or propositions.
- **Compression:** reducing surface material without losing required relation, evidence, voice, or rhythm.
- **Expansion:** adding surface material because the reader needs a relation, pressure, access cue, or concrete grounding that is currently missing.
- **Mediation:** linguistic packaging that places an unnecessary perceptual or abstract layer between stimulus and represented experience.
- **Sentence-local defect:** a failure whose cause and repair can be contained within one sentence without changing paragraph or scene architecture.

## SCOPE
Use this module when the material problem is primarily sentence-level: ambiguous reference, broken clause relation, unnecessary mediation, accidental repetition, ineffective compression/expansion, unintended rhythm, or unclear information order.

Do not use sentence craft to:
- rewrite a paragraph whose architecture already works;
- shorten a sentence merely because it is long;
- expand every summary into sensory showing;
- regularize fragments or marked syntax merely because they are nonstandard;
- invent a missing referent, fact, or causal relation.

## MECHANISM
### 1. Identify what the sentence must carry
Name the sentence's required work: event, decision, relation, perception, attribution, transition, pressure, or compression.

### 2. Track information order
Ask whether the current order preserves the intended emphasis and whether later elements depend on earlier ones.

### 3. Map clause relations
If a sentence contains multiple clauses, determine whether each changes information or merely repeats it. A long sentence can remain functional when each clause changes the active decision, relation, or consequence.

### 4. Resolve material reference failures locally
When a pronoun has more than one viable antecedent and the referent matters, replace only the ambiguous expression if the intended referent is supplied. If it is not supplied, HOLD rather than guess.

### 5. Test rhythm and markedness
Sentence length, fragments, ellipsis, repetition, and marked order are functional variables. Judge them against surrounding syntax and scene pressure.

### 6. Remove redundant mediation only when demonstrated
When wording such as `cảm thấy một cảm giác` duplicates perception and the context already supplies a concrete contact or response, remove the redundant layer. Do not generalize this into a sensory-density rule.

If no concrete contact, body location, garment, posture, movement, or other sensory fact is supplied, do not invent one merely to make the revision feel more grounded. Prefer a direct non-mediated sensation bounded to the facts already present. If the literal task requires a specific bodily/contact realization that the evidence does not supply, HOLD rather than manufacture it.

### 7. Choose the smallest useful intervention
If the defect is sentence-local, keep the repair sentence-local unless broader architecture is causally responsible.

## TEXTUAL SIGNALS
- pronouns with multiple plausible antecedents;
- clauses whose logical relation is unclear;
- repeated nominal/perceptual packaging such as `cảm thấy một cảm giác`;
- abrupt order that changes unintended emphasis;
- long sentences with either cumulative functional development or dead duplication;
- fragments/ellipsis that contrast with surrounding syntax;
- repetition with changing or unchanged pressure;
- punctuation that obscures rather than supports relation;
- unqualified certainty replacing attribution.

## EFFECTS
Sentence choices can control:
- emphasis and suspense;
- decision sequence;
- pace and breath;
- perceptual immediacy or distance;
- voice and social position;
- causal clarity;
- ambiguity or precision;
- reader effort and inference.

No single effect follows automatically from length, punctuation, or grammatical completeness.

## CONDITIONS
Preserve the sentence when:
- reference is recoverable at the required level;
- every clause contributes materially;
- marked rhythm serves pressure/voice/perception;
- compression does not hide required causality;
- ambiguity is intended or harmless.

Repair when:
- a reference failure changes material interpretation;
- clause relation is unintentionally opaque;
- mediation duplicates already available sensation/action;
- repetition adds no changed function;
- information order causes unintended misreading;
- sentence syntax creates a defect that the surrounding structure does not require.

## COUNTEREXAMPLES
- A 49-word sentence is not defective when each clause narrows the decision and reference remains clear.
- `Khói. Tai ù. Cửa ở đâu?` can be functional sentence fracture after impact.
- `Mai đưa chìa khóa cho Lan trước khi cô ký giấy bán nhà` requires local repair only if the signer matters and the intended referent is supplied.
- `Gió nóng quất vào mặt. Người anh cảm thấy một cảm giác nóng ran lan khắp cơ thể.` supports a narrow mediation/contact repair, not a global instruction to add imagery.
- A direct summary sentence can be preferable to dramatization when expansion would add no useful pressure.

## COMMON ERRORS
- treating word count as a quality score;
- assuming short sentences are clearer or stronger;
- expanding every abstract statement into sensory detail;
- smoothing functional fragments;
- replacing ambiguity by guessing the intended fact;
- changing a whole paragraph to repair one pronoun;
- adding imagery when the defect is mediation or reference;
- confusing punctuation preference with material improvement.

## HISTORICAL VARIATION
`SOURCE-GATED`.

Historical syntax, punctuation, orthography, and lexical order cannot be judged solely by contemporary prose expectations. Preserve uncertainty until period/source evidence is available.

## FORM VARIATION
`SOURCE-GATED BY FORM`.

Poetry, biền văn, regulated prose, oral-style narration, epistolary writing, and other forms may establish sentence or line relations different from modern prose norms. Do not import form-law from memory.

## CLOSE READING METHOD
Use:

`REQUIRED WORK → INFORMATION ORDER → CLAUSE RELATION → REFERENCE → RHYTHM/MARKEDNESS → MEDIATION → MATERIAL DEFECT? → ACTION`

If no material defect is demonstrated, preserve.

## CRAFT TRANSFER
Transfer sentence mechanisms rather than sentence shapes.

Examples:
- transfer a narrowing-decision sentence by changing event, syntax, and diction while preserving progressive constraint;
- transfer pressure-shaped fracture using a different sensory/event trigger;
- transfer direct contact by choosing a context-supported body/object contact rather than copying the same sensation.

## DRILLS
1. Diagnose five long sentences without using word count as the reason.
2. Repair three ambiguous pronouns using only supplied referents.
3. Compare functional repetition with dead duplication.
4. Remove redundant mediation without adding ornamental sensory detail.
5. Rebuild one sentence with a different information order and explain the changed emphasis.

## AI OPERATIONAL RULES
- Length alone is never a failure criterion.
- Do not normalize fragments, ellipsis, marked order, or repetition without a demonstrated defect.
- Resolve material reference ambiguity locally when the intended referent is supplied; otherwise HOLD.
- Do not convert claims or inferences into facts while compressing.
- Use `SENSORY-MEDIATION v0.1` only in its narrow contact-bound scope.
- Do not manufacture imagery to justify revision.
- Ground sensory repair only in supplied contact/body facts. When none are supplied, do not invent a body site, garment, contact, posture, movement, or other sensory fact just to make the line more concrete.
- A sentence-local defect should not trigger a structural rewrite unless the larger structure is causally responsible.

## SELF-CHECK
- What work must this sentence perform?
- Did I identify an actual defect or merely a preference?
- Does each clause change information or function?
- Is reference recoverable without guessing?
- Is the unusual rhythm functional against its context?
- Did compression alter source status or certainty?
- Did I expand sensation beyond supplied evidence?
- Can the repair remain sentence-local?
