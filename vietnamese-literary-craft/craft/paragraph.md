# KX5 — Paragraph Craft

**Status:** `CANDIDATE-STABLE / VALIDATED`

## CONCEPT
Paragraph craft controls how information, reference, time, causality, focus, and pressure develop across several sentences. Cohesion is a relation among sentences, not a quota of transition words.

Core route:

`FOCUS → REFERENCE CHAIN → PROGRESSION → INFORMATION DEVELOPMENT → PRESSURE/PACING → BREAK FUNCTION`

## DEFINITION
- **Paragraph focus:** the dominant local action, perception, question, relation, or informational task organizing the paragraph.
- **Reference chain:** the sequence by which entities, objects, claims, or topics remain identifiable across sentences.
- **Progression:** temporal, causal, logical, spatial, or associative movement from one sentence to the next.
- **Information development:** how new material is introduced, elaborated, contrasted, delayed, or resolved.
- **Paragraph break:** a boundary that can mark change in action, time, focal attention, speaker, pressure, or informational unit.
- **Local cohesion:** sufficient relational continuity for the reader to reconstruct how adjacent sentences belong together.

## SCOPE
Use this module when the issue concerns relations among multiple sentences: unclear progression, broken reference chain, accidental drift, paragraph break placement, pacing, or redundant connective scaffolding.

Do not use it to:
- add connectors merely because adjacent sentences are short;
- rewrite a paragraph whose chronology/reference already supplies sufficient cohesion;
- make every paragraph the same length;
- force one-topic purity when a pressure-shaped paragraph legitimately turns;
- hide sentence-level reference defects inside a structural rewrite.

## MECHANISM
### 1. Name the paragraph's local work
Ask what the paragraph must accomplish: execute an action sequence, develop a realization, stage a social exchange, compress time, build evidence, or shift pressure.

### 2. Trace the reference chain
Track who/what each sentence refers to. Repair only material breaks that make action or attribution unrecoverable.

### 3. Identify the progression relation
Chronology, causality, spatial continuity, contrast, and information development can create cohesion without explicit connective words.

### 4. Test information development
Each sentence should either move, qualify, deepen, oppose, reframe, or deliberately suspend the local focus. Repetition can remain when its changed function matters.

### 5. Test pressure and pacing
Sentence length and paragraph density may change with action, hesitation, processing, or scene pressure. Uniformity is not the goal.

### 6. Judge the break
A break is useful when it clarifies a material change in time, speaker, focus, pressure, scene beat, or information architecture. It is not required after a fixed sentence count.

### 7. Add a connector only if it solves an actual relation problem
Prefer the smallest scene-grounded bridge when the relation is not recoverable. Do not inflate connective prose when sequence/reference already does the work.

## TEXTUAL SIGNALS
- pronouns or objects whose antecedents drift across sentences;
- chronological sequences that are already self-ordering;
- causal jumps without supplied links;
- repetitive sentence openings or transitions;
- abrupt paragraph breaks at meaningful pressure changes;
- long paragraphs whose internal focus remains coherent;
- short paragraphs used for interruption, emphasis, or scene turn;
- generic connectors inserted where action order is already clear;
- a paragraph that changes scene question without signaling or function.

## EFFECTS
Paragraph organization can control:
- orientation and continuity;
- pace and pause;
- accumulation and release;
- suspense and delayed interpretation;
- attention to object/action/voice;
- reader inference;
- emphasis through break or density.

The same surface length or connector count can produce different effects under different scene functions.

## CONDITIONS
Preserve a paragraph when:
- reference chains remain recoverable;
- progression is reconstructable through order/context;
- sentence variation matches pressure or information development;
- the break pattern supports scene function;
- adding connectors would only restate relations the reader already has.

Repair when:
- a required causal/logical relation is missing;
- reference chains collapse materially;
- the paragraph drifts between unrelated functions without intentional effect;
- a break obscures who acts/speaks or when;
- connective language replaces concrete relation rather than clarifying it.

## COUNTEREXAMPLES
- `Khóa cửa → đếm tiền → giấu sổ → rời cửa sau` can cohere chronologically without `sau đó`, `tiếp theo`, or other explicit transitions.
- One long sentence followed by two fragments can form a coherent pressure peak.
- A paragraph can pause external action for interior processing without being structurally idle.
- A single ambiguous pronoun inside an otherwise working paragraph is a sentence-local defect, not evidence that the whole paragraph needs rewriting.
- A paragraph break can be effective after one sentence or after many; count alone proves nothing.

## COMMON ERRORS
- treating transition words as required cohesion markers;
- inserting generic connectors into already clear action sequences;
- equalizing paragraph lengths;
- splitting every long paragraph for readability without diagnosing its internal work;
- merging short paragraphs merely for visual balance;
- confusing causal absence with stylistic abruptness;
- rewriting architecture to fix one local pronoun;
- assuming every paragraph needs one explicit topic sentence in literary prose.

## HISTORICAL VARIATION
`SOURCE-GATED`.

Paragraphing, punctuation, and discourse segmentation vary across periods, editions, and textual traditions. Do not impose contemporary paragraph norms on older or editorially mediated texts without source evidence.

## FORM VARIATION
`SOURCE-GATED BY FORM`.

Poetry, epistolary prose, chronicles, dialogue-heavy fiction, biền văn, documentary fiction, and other forms may organize units differently. Form-specific segmentation rules require the relevant source/module.

## CLOSE READING METHOD
Use:

`LOCAL WORK → REFERENCE CHAIN → RELATION BETWEEN SENTENCES → INFORMATION CHANGE → PRESSURE/PACING → BREAK/CONNECTOR FUNCTION → ACTION`

If chronology, reference, and information development already make the relation clear, do not add connective prose merely to smooth.

## CRAFT TRANSFER
Transfer paragraph movement rather than paragraph length.

Examples:
- recreate an accumulating action sequence with different actions and syntax but the same progressive narrowing;
- recreate a pressure turn by shifting paragraph boundary at a different kind of decision point;
- recreate delayed interpretation by keeping one object present across sentences while changing its practical function.

## DRILLS
1. Remove unnecessary connectors from a clear chronological paragraph without losing orientation.
2. Diagnose whether five paragraph breaks are functional or merely visual.
3. Map reference chains across a paragraph with two same-gender characters.
4. Distinguish a productive abrupt transition from a missing causal link.
5. Rebuild paragraph movement with different sentence lengths and surface syntax.

## AI OPERATIONAL RULES
- Connectors are resources, never quotas.
- Do not add generic transitions when chronology/reference already carries cohesion.
- Paragraph length alone is not a failure criterion.
- Preserve pressure-shaped sentence/paragraph asymmetry when function is demonstrated.
- Repair causal or referential gaps only where evidence shows a material failure.
- Do not convert a sentence-local issue into a paragraph rewrite without causal necessity.
- Do not force literary prose into expository topic-sentence templates.

## SELF-CHECK
- What is this paragraph doing locally?
- Can the reader reconstruct who/what each sentence refers to?
- What relation links adjacent sentences?
- Is the relation already supplied by order/context?
- Did I add a connector because of a real gap or because the transition felt abrupt?
- Is the paragraph break carrying a change in focus/pressure/time?
- Did I flatten meaningful pacing variation?
